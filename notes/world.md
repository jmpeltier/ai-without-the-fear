# Instructor notes: Parts 7 and 8

## Part 7. What AI is and isn't (6 min)

Talking points:
- A chatbot is a well-read pattern machine. It is not a search engine, a person, or a crystal ball.
- "Hallucination" means the AI fills a gap with something that sounds right but isn't. Local example: in 2025 the Georgia Court of Appeals threw out a divorce ruling that cited court cases that don't exist (The Register, July 8, 2025).
- It sounds caring because it learned from billions of sentences people wrote about feelings. There is no good evidence it feels anything.
- Memory depends on the app and your settings. Most let you view, delete, or turn memory off, and many offer a temporary chat. The company may still keep your chats.
- Real concerns are calmer than "taking over the world": scams, fake voices and videos, privacy, bias, jobs, energy.
- The "sort of" cards are the teachable ones. Linger there.

Play-along moment: "Everyone tap your answer on card 1 before I read the answer." Do 3 or 4 cards together as a room, then let people finish alone. Then run "Spot the hallucination" together: ask for a show of hands on which sentence looks fake before anyone taps "Show me."

Likely question: "If it makes things up, why would I ever use it?"
Answer: Use it for jobs where you can check the result or where being a little off doesn't hurt: drafting a letter, explaining a word, summarizing something you have in front of you. Don't use it as the final word on facts, names, quotes, or book titles.

## Part 8. Why all the data centers? (7 min)

Talking points:
- A data center is a warehouse of computers, plus power and cooling. AI uses them twice: training (once, weeks to months on thousands of chips, like writing the cookbook) and answering (every question, all day, like cooking from it).
- Scale is the whole story. ChatGPT alone had 900 million weekly users (OpenAI, Feb. 2026) and about 2.5 billion questions a day (OpenAI, July 2025).
- One question is tiny: about 0.24 Wh (Google, Gemini median text prompt) or 0.34 Wh (OpenAI, average). Stress that these are company-reported and not independently checked. Images, video, and "thinking" models use more.
- Metro Atlanta is the second-largest data center market in the US (CBRE, end of 2025). On screen are two tiles. The rest (2,076 MW under construction, the tax break, Roswell's pause) is in "Read more: Georgia in detail".
- In Dec. 2025 the Georgia PSC approved nearly 10,000 MW of new generation for Georgia Power, mostly to serve data centers. The fight is over who pays if the demand doesn't show up.
- Stay neutral. "Two sides", "What can I do?" and the sources are folded for after class. If time allows, open "Read more: two sides" and read both columns aloud. Roswell itself paused new data centers in January 2026 to write rules, so people in the room may have strong views.

Play-along moment: "Slide the calculator to how many questions you think you'd ask in a month. Now look at the yellow box: that's everyone." Ask the room to compare their number to the microwave seconds.

Likely question: "Is my asking ChatGPT questions raising my power bill?"
Answer: Your own questions use a tiny amount of energy, about a second of a microwave each by the companies' own numbers. What affects Georgia bills is the total: new power plants built for data centers statewide. The PSC decides how those costs are split between data centers and households, and you can comment on those cases at psc.ga.gov/public-comments.
