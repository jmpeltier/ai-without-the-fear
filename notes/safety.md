# Instructor notes: safety sections

## Part 8. Try it safely (5 min)

- Big idea: a chatbot is like a helpful stranger at the library. Great for questions and letters, but you don't hand it your wallet.
- Anything typed into a chatbot may be stored on the company's computers. So the rule is simple: no passwords, account numbers, or Social Security numbers. Ever.
- "Careful" items, like medical results or a rash photo, are fine once you strip names, dates of birth, and ID numbers. Then check the answer with your doctor.
- The four big tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot) all have free versions and official apps. Show how to check the maker's name in the app store, because look-alike apps charge subscriptions.
- Settings: most apps have a switch under Settings, then Privacy or Data Controls, about using your chats to train the AI. The wording differs and changes, so the page describes it generically on purpose.
- Prompt recipe: who you are, what you want, how you want it.

**Play-along moment.** "Everyone take 90 seconds and sort the eight items. Shout out the one that surprised you." Then have them tap Copy on one starter prompt. Anyone with a chatbot app can paste it in.

**Likely question.** "If I already typed something private, is it out there forever?"
Answer: Not public, but it may be stored. Delete that chat from your history. If it was a password, change the password. If it was a card number, call your bank.

## Part 9. Spot the AI scam (7 min)

- Big idea: AI makes old scams more convincing. A cloned voice, a fake celebrity video, a perfectly written email. The tricks underneath (fear, hurry, secrecy, strange payment) haven't changed.
- The FBI's December 2024 alert says criminals clone a loved one's voice from short clips and make fake profile photos and celebrity videos. Its first tip is a family secret word.
- Perfect spelling no longer proves an email is real.
- No real business or government agency asks for gift cards (FTC). Gift cards, crypto, and wire transfers are scammers' favorites because the money is nearly impossible to get back.
- Two of the eight stories are real, so the room can't just answer "scam" every time. The two real ones teach the habit: you started it, or they passed the safe word.
- Be kind about victims. These are professionals. Reporting helps.

**Play-along moment.** Read story 1 (the grandson call) aloud with feeling, then have everyone vote on their phones. After the quiz, everyone taps "Make me a safe word" and reads theirs out. It gets laughs and it sticks.

**Likely question.** "What if my grandkid forgets the safe word in a real emergency?"
Answer: Then hang up and call them, or their parents, on the number you already have. The safe word is a bonus check. Calling back is the real safety net.

## Part 10. Take-home cheat sheet (3 min)

- Walk the seven ideas quickly, one sentence each. They recap every part of the class.
- The glossary is for later. Point out that it's there rather than reading it.
- Show the Print button. It prints just this cheat sheet, without the menus. Good for the fridge or for a friend who missed class.
- The questions box saves on their own phone only. Nothing is sent anywhere.
- Close by noting the page stays online. They can replay the games and make new safe words anytime.

**Play-along moment.** "Type one question you still have into the box at the bottom. Bring it next time, or ask me now."

**Likely question.** "Can I share this with my sister who couldn't come?"
Answer: Yes. Send her the web address, or print the cheat sheet. Nothing needs a sign-up.
